### Front Office inventory and reservations (2026-10-01)

- Status: `TASK-FO-002`, `TASK-FO-005` and `TASK-FO-007` are `REVIEW`; `TASK-FO-003` and `TASK-FO-004` are `IN_PROGRESS`. Reasons: a **guaranteed** reservation needs a deposit and comes with the payment tasks (`TASK-FO-024`, `-025`), so `FO-003` offers tentative and confirmed only; the **penalty** rules of a late cancellation or a no-show are policy per rate plan and source (`TASK-FO-009`), so `FO-004` records the cancellation or no-show with its reason but applies no penalty yet. Nothing here consumes room blocks from Maintenance (the Maintenance context will call the same block service). Moving a reservation to another room type or dates is not built.
- Traceability: `FR-FO-002`, `-003`, `-004`, `-005`, `-007`; `BR-001`, `BR-003`, `BR-005`, `BR-006`, `BR-007`, `BR-009`, `BR-011`, `NFR-17`, `NFR-18`, `NFR-19`, `NFR-27`; `docs/ARCHITECTURE/03`, `10`.
- Inventory model: rooms of a type that can be sold on a night = active rooms, minus rooms out of order or out of service, minus allotments and holds still active, minus rooms promised to reservations that hold inventory (`reservation_nights`, one row per room night, flagged inactive when a reservation is cancelled or no-show but kept as history). A negative result is an **oversold night**, shown as such. The horizon of the calendar is a property setting (default 365 days).
- Booking rules (`ReservationService`): idempotent creation with an `Idempotency-Key` (a changed payload under the same key is refused); arrival not before the business date and within the sales horizon; guests within the room type's occupancy; priced by the rate plan and **fail-closed** (a missing price, an unconfigured service charge and tax scheme, a stopped or closed date, minimum or maximum stay each name the reason); the room type is locked while availability is decided and the reservation is stored; reservation numbers come from a never-reused per-property sequence (`RSV-000001`, `BR-006`); the price is kept as an immutable snapshot (nights, base, service charge, tax, and the scheme in force on each night), enforced by a database trigger, together with a total check; reservations are never deleted (trigger) and a cancellation or no-show needs a reason (check constraint). No-show is only possible from the arrival date by the business date.
- Overbooking (`BR-007`): the default sells nothing beyond the physical rooms. A property sets an allowance per room type (audited, needs `front-office.overbooking.manage` and a reason). A booking that would use it is refused with a warning until the person acknowledges it, holds `front-office.overbooking.override`, and gives a reason; the reservation is flagged oversold with that reason. Beyond the allowance it is refused outright.
- Room blocks and holds: out of order and out of service blocks with a date range and reason (overlaps on one room are refused; releasing needs a reason; the response lists nights that become oversold because reservations already exist), and allotments or holds of a number of rooms of a type, refused when fewer rooms are free, with an optional expiry after which the rooms go back on sale by themselves. Rows are append-only apart from a one-time release.
- Privacy (`BR-009`): the guest's phone and e-mail are returned only to people who may see them (`front-office.guest-contact.view` or reservation management), never in lists, never in audit entries or outbox events, and each reading of them on a reservation is recorded as a personal data access.
- UI (English and Indonesian): `/front-office/availability` (calendar per room type with the number left, text and shape for sold out and oversold, and the selling markers of a chosen rate plan), `/front-office/reservations` (search and filters, a booking dialog with a price and availability check, a server-driven oversell acknowledgement), `/front-office/reservations/{id}` (detail, price snapshot, confirm, cancel and no-show with consequence dialogs) and `/front-office/inventory` (blocks, holds, overbooking allowance).
- A defect found by running a real race: with the room type locked, 4 of 12 simultaneous bookings for the single room succeeded. MySQL's default isolation let a booking that had waited for the lock decide on the counts of the snapshot it took before waiting. The counts used for a decision now come from locking reads; the same 12-way race (three runs, on a multi-process server, one of them with a 400 ms delay between the decision and the insert to widen the window) leaves exactly one reservation. Availability on the calendar and in the price check stays a plain read.

New: 15 MySQL integration tests (`ReservationTest`: numbering, pricing, snapshot, audit without guest data, outbox, idempotent replay and conflict, last room, adjacency, overbooking with every guard, booking rules and bad input, cancellation and its guards, no-show timing, confirmation, blocks with oversold report and overlap and release and permission, holds with expiry, contact masking and audit, search filters with literal wildcards and property scope, database guards, document numbers and rollback) and 7 HTTP tests (`ReservationHttpTest`). Browser evidence in real Chromium on a multi-process server: creating a reservation with the price check, the calendar, a refused second booking, the allowance, the oversell warning flow and its reason, the flag on the detail page, cancellation with a reason, a room block and its effect on the calendar, the Indonesian UI; no page errors.
- Not included: guaranteed reservations and deposits, penalties, room assignment and check-in (`TASK-FO-010`), group bookings (`TASK-FO-006`), the room board (`TASK-FO-001`), changing a booking's dates or room type, channel manager sync, and automatic release of tentative bookings after an option date.

### Folio ledger, payments, deposits and approved corrections (2026-10-01)

- Status: `TASK-FO-024`, `-025` and `-029` are `REVIEW`; `TASK-FO-020` is `IN_PROGRESS`. `FO-020` asks that every **stay** has at least one folio; stays and check-in are not built yet, so a folio is opened on a reservation (several windows are supported) and charges from outlets and laundry will arrive through the same ledger when those modules exist. `FO-024` records cash, QRIS, card (EDC reference), bank transfer and online-channel payments **by staff entry with the slip or transfer reference**; there is no payment gateway integration (PRD `Q-04`, `Q-16` are open), so a QRIS payment is confirmed by a person, never by a callback. A foreign-currency payment (`FO-026`, optional) is not supported. Posting room charges every night belongs to night audit (`TASK-FO-028`).
- Traceability: `FR-FO-020`, `-024`, `-025`, `-029`; `BR-002`, `BR-003`, `BR-004`, `BR-005`, `BR-006`, `BR-011`, `NFR-06`, `NFR-09`, `NFR-10`, `NFR-18`, `NFR-19`, `NFR-22`; `docs/ARCHITECTURE/08`, `09`.
- Ledger design: `folios` are headers; the truth is `folio_postings`, an append-only, ordered list (gapless sequence per folio) of **charges** (base, service charge and tax kept separately, with the scheme in force), **payments** (method and reference; a deposit is a payment with purpose `deposit`), **refunds** and **reversals**. Update and delete are refused by database triggers; check constraints reject a charge whose parts do not add up, a negative charge, a positive payment, a payment without a method, a reversal or refund without a reason, and a second reversal of the same posting. A stored balance moves under the folio's row lock, and a test replays 40 random operations asserting that it always equals the sum of the ledger and that sequence numbers have no gaps. Derived statuses: open, partially settled, settled; `closed` is explicit and then refuses every posting. A deposit before any charge simply leaves a credit that later charges consume, and the unused part is refunded.
- Idempotency (`BR-005`): a posting that carries a source and reference (the screen's `Idempotency-Key`, later night audit and outlets) is written once however many times it is delivered; the same reference with different content is a conflict. Folio and document numbers (`FOL-000001`) are never reused and a refused attempt consumes none.
- Corrections (`BR-003`, `FR-FO-029`): a charge on an open folio is corrected by a **reversal that mirrors it exactly** (parts negated), with a reason, once, on the current business date; a reversal cannot be reversed; the original is untouched. A **refund**, the reversal of a **payment or refund**, and any correction on a **settled** folio need an approved request (`ApprovalGate`, subjects `front-office.folio.reversal` and `front-office.folio.refund`, both mandatory): with no policy configured they fail closed; the person who asked is the only one who can use the approval, once, for exactly the posting and amount that were approved; the posting and the audit entry carry the approval reference. Changing, closing, reversing and refunding need a recent password confirmation.
- Approval policy administration (new, needed for the above): `/approvals/policies` lists every declared subject with whether it is mandatory and the current chains, and sets a new version (band from an amount, up to five steps, permission and number of approvals each, reason, audited). Without it a mandatory subject could never be used.
- Card data (`NFR-09`): a payment reference accepts only a short slip, approval or transfer reference; anything that looks like a card number (13 to 19 digits, even inside text) is refused.
- UI (English and Indonesian): `/front-office/folios/{id}` shows balance (owed, credit or settled), charges and payments, every posting with base, service charge, tax and total, reversed postings marked, and offers post a charge (digits-only amount, "++" or nett), record a payment, reverse, refund and close with consequence dialogs; the reservation page lists its folios and opens one. A reversal that needs approval shows why, offers to request it, shows when it is pending or approved, and uses the approved request automatically.
- Automated evidence: PHPUnit 493 tests (1 skipped, pre-existing), `npm test` 89 tests. New: 15 MySQL integration tests (`FolioTest`: numbering and windows, the exact split of charges, idempotent postings and conflicts, every payment method and the derived status, payment validation and card numbers, deposits, closing rules and a closed folio, mirror reversals once, fail-closed and single-use approvals tied to the maker, settled folios, refunds for the exact amount, permissions and scope, the database guards, and the randomized balance invariant) and 4 HTTP tests (`FolioHttpTest`). Mutation checks confirmed the tests fail when approval is skipped, when the double-reversal check is removed (the database unique key still catches it) and when a closed folio accepts postings. Browser evidence in real Chromium on a multi-process server with two signed-in users: a settled folio, a charge split correctly, a card number refused, a payment, the approval-required flow for reversing a cash payment, the supervisor approving it in the inbox, the requester using it once, the reversed posting shown, the Indonesian UI; no page errors.
- Not included: stays and check-in (`TASK-FO-010`), automatic posting of room charges (`TASK-FO-028`), outlet and laundry charges (their modules), split bill and moving items between folios (`FO-022`, `-023`), the printed bill (`FO-021`), late charges after closing (`FO-038`), foreign currency, payment gateways and settlement reconciliation, shift cash reconciliation (`FO-036`), and posting revenue to Finance (`FO-027`; the outbox events `frontoffice.folio.*` are published for it).

- Check-in (`StayService`, route `/front-office/reservations/{id}/check-in`): only a confirmed or guaranteed reservation, on or after its arrival date and before its departure date by the **business date**; a room that is active, of the booked room type, has no in-house guest and is not out of order or out of service; guests within the room type's occupancy. In one transaction under the room type's lock it records the guest, the stay, moves the reservation to `checked_in` with the room, opens the first folio when none exists, and writes the audit entry, a personal data access entry and the `frontoffice.stay.checked_in` outbox event. A test asserts the raw rows, audit entries and outbox payloads contain neither the number nor the address. Stay screens show the number masked to the last four characters and no address unless the person holds `front-office.guest-identity.view`; each such reading is a personal data access audit entry. Guests cannot be deleted (trigger); erasure goes through the retention process.
- Returning guests (`FO-015`): looking up a number returns the names of earlier registrations with the same document (number normalized without spaces, hyphens or dots; another document type is another document) with how many stays and the last one, and records a personal data access entry. Names only, no document details.
- Document warnings (`FO-014`): expired, expires before departure, or a foreign guest without a visa number (KITAS holders excepted). They inform and never block; the front desk decides.
- Identity photo (`FO-011`): JPEG or PNG up to 5 MB, content inspected rather than trusted by extension, stored encrypted as a `sensitive` private file with purpose `guest.identity`, owned by the stay. It is served only through an audited download that requires the identity permission, with `no-store` and `nosniff`. Replacing a photo expires the old one at once so the daily purge erases it. **Retention starts at check-out**: the file's expiry is set once to the check-out business date plus the property's `guest_identity_document` retention (default 90 days, maximum 365; see `FND-019`). To allow that, migration 19 replaces the file-row trigger: besides the erasure tombstone, an expiry may be set exactly once from none to a date; every other field stays fixed (tested, including a second change being refused).

A stale version is refused, and a completed stay cannot change (trigger).

New: 12 MySQL integration tests (`StayTest`: check-in effects, encryption at rest and no leakage into audit or outbox, masking and audited reading, check-in rules and atomic rollback, occupied and blocked rooms, database guards, idempotent retry, warnings, returning guest, check-out with balance gate and early departure and photo retention, photo rules and replacement, set-once expiry) and 4 HTTP tests (`StayHttpTest`). ### Night audit (2026-10-01)

- Status: `TASK-FO-028` is `REVIEW`. The requirement lists "pre-check pending transactions"; today the checks cover Front Office items (below). Pending items of outlets, laundry, approvals and Housekeeping join the same list when those contexts exist; nothing is assumed about them. The report is the Front Office day report (occupancy, arrivals, departures, revenue split into base, service charge and tax, money collected by method); the accounting handoff is the outbox event `frontoffice.night_audit.completed` for `FO-027` (not built). PRD `Q-11` is answered by the Indonesian baseline (`INDONESIA-COMPLIANCE-BASELINE.md`): an earliest local start (property setting, default 23:00) and no latest time; counsel is not needed, the General Manager confirms.
- Traceability: `FR-FO-028`, `BR-001`, `BR-002`, `BR-003`, `BR-005`, `NFR-18`, `NFR-26`; `docs/ARCHITECTURE/07`, `12`.
- Rules (`NightAuditService`, route `/front-office/night-audit`): it closes the property's **current business date** and nothing else. It cannot start before the earliest time of that date in the **property's time zone**; the clock never starts it, a person with `front-office.night-audit.run` does, and it needs a recent password confirmation and an `Idempotency-Key`. Everything happens in one transaction: a failure leaves the day, the postings and the date untouched (tested with a failing step).
- Checks: arrivals not yet resolved (tentative, confirmed or guaranteed with an arrival date up to today), in-house guests whose booked departure is today or earlier, in-house guests with no open folio, and stays that began and ended today (the night was never charged; the price of a day-use stay is a policy nobody has decided, so it is flagged, not guessed). A pending check **blocks** the run unless someone holding `front-office.night-audit.waive` waives that check with a reason; the waivers, their counts and reasons are stored with the record and in the audit entry. A person without the privilege cannot waive.
- Room charges: one charge per in-house stay for the night, taken from the **price snapshot** of the reservation (BR-002, a later rate change does not touch it), with base, service charge and tax as quoted then, posted to the first open folio under source `night_audit` and reference `reservationId:date`. The ledger's source-reference rule makes a retry, or a second attempt after a crash, reuse the existing posting; a test pre-posts a night and shows the audit does not duplicate it. A night outside the booked stay (a guest staying past the booked departure) is not charged a price nobody agreed; the overdue-departure check surfaces it.
- Report: computed from the ledger itself, not from counters: revenue of the date is the sum of charges and the reversals of charges with their parts, split into room (night audit) and other, and money collected per method; a test compares it with an independent sum over the postings, including a reversed minibar charge and a laundry charge. Each night is stored once per property (unique key) as an immutable record (update and delete refused by trigger).
- Day lock (`BR-001`): the business date is moved by `BusinessDateAdvancer`, whose only user is night audit (an architecture test lists every class that mentions it); it moves exactly one day forward and a database trigger already refuses moving it back. A trigger on `folio_postings` refuses any posting dated on or before a closed night; the application always posts to the current date, so the trigger is a backstop that a test exercises directly.
- UI (English and Indonesian): `/front-office/night-audit` (date, earliest start, whether the time has come, the checks with their items and a reason field for waivers, run with a consequence dialog, previous audits) and `/front-office/night-audit/{date}` (the report).
- Automated evidence: PHPUnit 523 tests (1 skipped, pre-existing).
